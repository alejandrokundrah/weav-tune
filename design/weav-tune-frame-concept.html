Design rationale / specification

Why this layout feels familiar

Beauty lives in the surface; usability lives in the structure. Every decision below maps to a behaviour listeners already have from Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music and the phone in their hand.

Seven habit-first principles

01 / ORIENTATION

Never lose your place

A persistent left nav, top search and bottom now-playing bar stay fixed. The full-screen player becomes an expandable sheet, so browsing while listening is always possible.

Habit: people keep browsing while a track plays.
02 / FINDABILITY

Search where eyes go first

One search field, always in the top bar, reachable with Ctrl/Cmd K. Recent searches and browse categories appear before a single character is typed.

Habit: search is the fastest route to a known song.
03 / REACH

Thumb-first on mobile

Four labelled tabs at the bottom, mini player directly above them, transport controls in the lower third of the expanded player. No critical action lives at the top of the screen.

Habit: one-handed listening on the move.
04 / CLARITY

Recognition over recall

Icons always paired with text in navigation, one consistent 24px icon set, explicit play buttons on hover, 44px minimum touch targets and visible focus rings.

Habit: users scan labels, they do not memorise glyphs.
05 / LEGIBILITY

Readable before beautiful

Glass is limited to floating chrome. Content and lyrics sit on opaque surfaces with at least 4.5:1 contrast, 13px minimum body text, and no text over artwork.

Habit: lyrics are read at a glance, often while singing.
06 / DEPTH

Power one layer down

Equalizer, AutoEq, crossfade, normalization, lossless add-ons and quality live in one Sound sheet. Casual listeners never see audiophile controls unless they ask.

Habit: settings are visited rarely, and deliberately.
07 / FORGIVENESS

Undo everything

Removing a queue item or clearing a playlist shows an Undo toast. Back is always visible, Esc closes overlays, and motion respects prefers-reduced-motion.

Habit: people explore by tapping and expect a way back.

Audit: what changed and why

Friction in the current UIWhy it fights a habitFrame solution
Full-screen player with tiny "Playing from" labelPlayback takes over the whole screen; navigation and back affordance disappear.Persistent bottom bar + an expandable sheet with an obvious down-chevron.
Lyrics in translucent brown on a brown artwork washFails contrast; active line is hard to track at a glance while singing along.Opaque lyrics panel, 20px lines, highlighted active line at ~7:1 contrast.
Heavy glass and animated aura on every surfaceCompetes with content, costs GPU, and reduces text contrast on long lists.Glass only on top bar, player bar, mini player and tab bar; content is solid.
Mood-board grid of unlabelled colour tilesBeautiful but ambiguous: users cannot tell what a tile does or what it plays.Titled cards with artwork, source label and an explicit Play button on hover.
Transport controls far from the thumb on mobileForces two-handed use for the most frequent action in a music app.Transport in the lower third; 62px play button; tabs within thumb arc.
Pro audio features scattered through SettingsCasual listeners face audiophile terms; experts cannot find them in one place.One Sound sheet with quality, EQ, normalization, crossfade and add-ons.

Accessibility checklist

Body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast in both themes; active lyric line ≥ 7:1.
All interactive elements ≥ 44px with a visible 3px focus ring.
Icon + text labels in all primary navigation; aria-labels on icon-only controls.
prefers-reduced-motion and prefers-contrast:more remove blur and animation.
Keyboard-only path for search, playback, seeking and closing overlays.
Undo available for every destructive queue / playlist action.
